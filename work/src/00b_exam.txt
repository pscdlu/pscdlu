::front This unit in the examination
::h How many questions, and how they are marked
Paper 1 has 50 compulsory questions. Each carries 2 marks, so the paper is out of 100. There is no negative marking, so never leave a question blank. Paper 1 and Paper 2 are taken together in one sitting of three hours, as a computer-based test.
The syllabus has ten units, and the paper is designed to take **five questions from each unit**. So Teaching Aptitude is worth about 10 marks. Sometimes a sixth question touches this unit too, because a topic such as SWAYAM or classroom communication also belongs to the units on ICT, Communication or Higher Education.
::h The formats you will meet
The paper no longer asks only plain one-answer questions. In recent papers a large share of questions use these formats:
- **Multiple-select.** Five numbered statements; you choose the option that lists the correct ones, such as "1, 3 and 4 only".
- **Match the list.** Four items in List I to be matched with four in List II; you choose the option with the right set of pairs.
- **Sequencing.** Four or five steps or stages to be put in order, such as the levels of Bloom's taxonomy or Piaget's stages.
- **Two statements.** Statement I and Statement II; you decide whether each is true or false.
- **Assertion and Reason.** You decide whether each statement is true, and then whether the Reason explains the Assertion.
- **Classroom scenarios.** A short situation, and the question asks what the teacher should do or what kind of method or evaluation is being used.
Every "Check yourself" in this guide uses these formats, with more room given to the harder ones.
::h The topics that recur most
A survey of what candidates have recalled from the papers set by NTA since December 2018, and of what the standard Paper 1 books treat as core, shows these topics coming back again and again, roughly in this order:
1. Types of evaluation: formative and summative, norm-referenced and criterion-referenced, often as a classroom scenario (Section 6.3).
2. Bloom's taxonomy and the domains of objectives (Section 1.4).
3. SWAYAM, SWAYAM PRABHA and MOOCs (Section 4.6).
4. Teacher-centred and learner-centred methods (Section 4.1).
5. The levels of teaching: memory, understanding and reflective (Section 1.5).
6. Learning theories and the learning process (Section 2.3).
7. Effective teaching and "what should the teacher do" scenarios (Section 1.7).
8. Group and cooperative methods such as seminar, symposium, panel and workshop (Section 4.3).
9. Piaget, Vygotsky and other thinkers on cognitive development (Section 2.2).
10. Micro-teaching and Flanders' Interaction Analysis (Section 1.8).
11. The Choice Based Credit System and grading (Section 6.7).
12. The nature of teaching and models of teaching (Sections 1.1 and 1.9).
Each section of the guide opens with an **Exam footprint** line that tells you how often its topic is asked and in what form. Spend your time accordingly, but do not skip the rarer sections: the booklet, and sometimes the examination, will ask about them.
