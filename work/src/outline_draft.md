# Draft chapter outline (section numbers fixed once approved)

Syllabus Unit I bullets, in order:
(a) Teaching: Concept, Objectives, Levels of teaching (Memory, Understanding and Reflective), Characteristics and basic requirements.
(b) Learner's characteristics: Characteristics of adolescent and adult learners (Academic, Social, Emotional and Cognitive), Individual differences.
(c) Factors affecting teaching related to: Teacher, Learner, Support material, Instructional facilities, Learning environment and Institution.
(d) Methods of teaching in Institutions of higher learning: Teacher centred vs. Learner centred methods; Off-line vs. On-line methods (Swayam, Swayamprabha, MOOCs etc.).
(e) Teaching Support System: Traditional, Modern and ICT based.
(f) Evaluation Systems: Elements and Types of evaluation, Evaluation in Choice Based Credit System in Higher education, Computer based testing, Innovations in evaluation systems.

## Chapter 1  Teaching: Concept, Objectives, Levels, Characteristics and Basic Requirements
1.1 Concept of teaching: definitions (Gage, Bigge, others), teaching and learning, teaching vs instruction, training, indoctrination, conditioning; teaching as art and science; teaching as a tripolar/bipolar process
1.2 Teaching as a process: phases (pre-active, interactive, post-active; Jackson), teaching variables (independent, dependent, intervening), operations of teaching
1.3 Objectives of teaching: aims, goals, objectives, learning outcomes; instructional objectives in behavioural terms (Mager, Tyler); general vs specific objectives
1.4 Taxonomies of educational objectives: Bloom cognitive (1956) and revised (Anderson & Krathwohl 2001, knowledge dimension); affective (Krathwohl, Bloom & Masia 1964); psychomotor (Simpson, Dave, Harrow); action verbs
1.5 Levels of teaching: memory (Herbart), understanding (Morrison), reflective (Hunt / Bigge); steps, teacher and learner roles, evaluation at each level; relation to Bloom
1.6 Maxims and principles of teaching
1.7 Characteristics of effective teaching and of a good teacher; teacher behaviour, leadership styles in class (Lewin), classroom management (Kounin)
1.8 Basic requirements of teaching: teacher competencies and teaching skills; micro-teaching (Allen, Stanford; Indian model, Passi; cycle and timings); simulated teaching; Flanders' Interaction Analysis
1.9 Models of teaching (Joyce & Weil families; advance organiser model, concept attainment, inquiry training, etc.) and theories of teaching
1.10 Professional ethics of the teacher (only as far as Unit I questions need)

## Chapter 2  Learner's Characteristics
2.1 The learner in higher education: adolescence and early adulthood as stages (Hall, Erikson, Marcia, Arnett, Levinson); who the adult learner is
2.2 Cognitive characteristics: Piaget (stages, schema, assimilation, accommodation, formal operations), Vygotsky (ZPD, scaffolding), Bruner (modes, spiral curriculum, discovery), Ausubel (meaningful learning, advance organisers), attention, memory, metacognition, critical thinking
2.3 How learners learn: theories of learning (Pavlov, Thorndike, Skinner, Köhler, Bandura, Gagné, constructivism), transfer of learning
2.4 Academic characteristics: learning styles (Kolb, VARK and others), study habits, self-regulated learning, achievement, aptitude
2.5 Social characteristics: peer group, identity, moral development (Kohlberg), cooperation and group work
2.6 Emotional characteristics: emotions, anxiety, emotional intelligence (Salovey & Mayer, Goleman), self-concept; motivation (intrinsic/extrinsic, Maslow, others)
2.7 Adult learners: andragogy (Knowles), heutagogy, pedagogy vs andragogy; Rogers' facilitation of learning
2.8 Individual differences: meaning, areas, causes; intelligence (Binet, IQ, Spearman, Thurstone, Guilford, Cattell, Sternberg, Gardner); aptitude, interest, attitude
2.9 Learners with diverse needs: gifted, slow learners, learning disabilities, learners with disabilities (RPwD Act 2016), inclusive education; educational implications of individual differences

## Chapter 3  Factors Affecting Teaching
3.1 Overview: the six groups of factors named in the syllabus
3.2 Teacher-related factors (knowledge, skills, personality, expectations, Pygmalion effect)
3.3 Learner-related factors
3.4 Support material
3.5 Instructional facilities
3.6 Learning environment: physical, psychological and social; classroom climate; Hawthorne and halo effects
3.7 Institution-related factors

## Chapter 4  Methods of Teaching in Institutions of Higher Learning
4.1 Method, approach, strategy, technique, tactic; how methods are classified; teacher-centred vs learner-centred
4.2 Teacher-centred methods: lecture, demonstration, lecture-cum-demonstration, programmed instruction (linear, branching), personalised system of instruction
4.3 Group and interactive methods: discussion, seminar, symposium, panel discussion, conference, workshop, tutorial, brainstorming, team teaching, peer tutoring, cooperative learning (jigsaw etc.)
4.4 Learner-centred and activity methods: project, problem-solving, inquiry, heuristic, case study, role play, simulation, Dalton Plan, Keller Plan, field work, mastery learning
4.5 Off-line vs on-line teaching: e-learning, synchronous/asynchronous, blended learning, flipped classroom
4.6 MOOCs and SWAYAM; SWAYAM PRABHA; other national digital initiatives (NPTEL, e-PG Pathshala, NDLI, Shodhganga, Virtual Labs, DIKSHA etc.); UGC credit transfer

## Chapter 5  Teaching Support System
5.1 Meaning, purposes and classification of teaching aids (audio, visual, audio-visual; projected, non-projected); Edgar Dale's Cone of Experience
5.2 Traditional support: chalkboard, charts, graphs, maps, models, specimens, flannel board, bulletin board, printed material
5.3 Modern support: OHP, slide and film projectors, radio, television, audio and video recordings, language laboratory
5.4 ICT-based support: computers, multimedia and PowerPoint, interactive boards, LMS, e-content and OER, virtual classrooms and labs

## Chapter 6  Evaluation Systems
6.1 Elements of evaluation: measurement, assessment, evaluation; the evaluation triangle (objectives, learning experiences, evaluation); functions of evaluation
6.2 Qualities of a good test: validity, reliability, objectivity, usability, norms
6.3 Types of evaluation: formative, summative, diagnostic, placement; norm- and criterion-referenced; internal and external; assessment for/of/as learning
6.4 Tools and techniques: types of test items, blueprint, essay vs objective, rating scales, observation; item analysis
6.5 Reading test scores: mean, median, standard deviation, percentile, standard scores, correlation (method chapter: worked computations)
6.6 Continuous and Comprehensive Evaluation; grading (absolute and relative)
6.7 Evaluation in the Choice Based Credit System: credits, letter grades and grade points, SGPA and CGPA, and the current frameworks (CCFUP 2022, NHEQF, NCrF, Academic Bank of Credits)
6.8 Computer-based testing and computer adaptive testing; item banks
6.9 Innovations in evaluation: rubrics, portfolios, open-book, online and on-demand examinations, self and peer assessment, question banks, 360-degree feedback, examination reforms
