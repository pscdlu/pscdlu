### S6-Q1  [key D]
Role play is especially useful for developing:

(A) speed of mental calculation	(B) accuracy of recall

(C) neatness of drawing	(D) empathy and social skills
EXPLANATION: (D),** because by acting as another person, a learner experiences that person's situation and feelings and practises ways of communicating. Role play is therefore valued for attitudes, empathy and interpersonal skills, which belong to the affective domain, more than for factual knowledge.

### S6-Q2  [key D]
In Morrison's model, the step in which pupils study the unit by themselves under the teacher's supervision is:

(A) exploration	(B) presentation

(C) recitation	(D) assimilation
EXPLANATION: (D),** because assimilation is the longest step of Morrison's model. Pupils work through the material individually in a supervised study period until they have mastered it, and the teacher tests and re-teaches as needed. Mastery is checked before the class moves on to organisation and recitation.

EXPLANATION: (A),** because an anecdotal record is a teacher's brief written description of a significant incident in a student's behaviour. It gives descriptive, not numerical, information. Test scores, percentile ranks and standard scores are quantitative. Evaluation makes use of both kinds of evidence.

### S6-Q4  [key C]

(A) Abraham Maslow	(B) Carl Rogers

EXPLANATION: (C),** because McClelland studied the need for achievement (n-Ach), the desire to do things well and to succeed against a standard of excellence. People high in this need prefer tasks of moderate difficulty and want feedback. Maslow proposed the hierarchy of needs.

### S6-Q5  [key C]
Vygotsky's approach to learning is described as:

(A) cognitive constructivism	(B) radical behaviourism

(C) social constructivism	(D) classical humanism
EXPLANATION: (C),** because both Piaget and Vygotsky are constructivists, holding that learners build knowledge. Piaget stressed the individual child acting on the world (cognitive constructivism), whereas Vygotsky stressed interaction with others and with culture, so his view is called social or socio-cultural constructivism.

EXPLANATION: (D),** because the classical formula is IQ = (mental age ÷ chronological age) × 100. Here 12 ÷ 10 = 1.2, and 1.2 × 100 = 120. Dividing the other way round, 10 ÷ 12, gives about 83, which is the usual slip. An IQ of 100 means mental age equals chronological age.
