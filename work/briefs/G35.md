## 3.2 Factors related to the teacher  (7 booklet questions)

### Core must teach
- S1-Q43 [single] Rosenthal and Jacobson (1968) showed pupils expected to bloom gained more; the Pygmalion effect is a self-fulfilling prophecy of teacher expectations.
- S2-Q7 [single] Six factor groups: teacher, learner, support material, instructional facilities, learning environment, institution. Communication skill, subject mastery, personality are teacher factors; home background is learner; timetable is institutional.
- S3-Q22 [single] A warm, enthusiastic teacher arouses interest and effort and builds trust; personality is a teacher-related factor along with subject mastery and communication skill.
- S5-Q47 [single] Clear explanation depends on subject mastery and communication/language skill (teacher factors); age, service length, status, handwriting and dress have no direct bearing.
- S9-Q17 [multi] Teacher expectations work as self-fulfilling prophecy (Pygmalion); questioning skill affects participation; subject knowledge is necessary but not sufficient.

### Going Further must teach
- S6-Q36 [single] Teacher efficacy = confidence in helping even difficult students learn; autonomy = professional freedom; accountability = answerability; burnout = exhaustion from stress.
- S11-Q47 [scenario] The Golem effect: low teacher expectations lead to treatment (less wait time, fewer hints) that lowers performance. The Pygmalion effect is the positive version (high expectations raise performance). Halo effect is a rating bias from one trait; Hawthorne effect is change due to being observed.

## 3.3 Factors related to the learner  (9 booklet questions)

### Core must teach
- S1-Q23 [single] Learner factors include prior knowledge, which shapes reception of new material. Subject mastery is a teacher factor, library is an institutional facility, seating layout is learning environment.
- S3-Q12 [single] New learning must link to what learners already know; previous knowledge or entry behaviour tells the teacher where to begin and which examples to use.
- S3-Q49 [single] Learner characteristics are cognitive (intelligence, aptitude, previous knowledge), affective (interest, attitude, motivation, values) and psychomotor; interest is affective.
- S5-Q13 [single] Maturation is the unfolding of physical and mental capacities with age, independent of training; learners must be mature enough (readiness) for teaching to succeed. Coaching, discipline and testing are external influences.
- S7-Q12 [scenario] Learner factors include cognitive, physical, emotional and social; anxiety is emotional and consumes attention and working memory; institutional factors lie outside the learner.
- S8-Q4 [two-statement] New material is interpreted through prior knowledge (Ausubel); physical condition such as fatigue reduces attention and efficiency of learning.

### Going Further must teach
- S13-Q21 [scenario] Cultural capital (Bourdieu) is knowledge, language habits and familiarity with educated culture passed on by families and rewarded by institutions; first-generation students lack exposure, not ability. Bridge courses and mentoring help.
- S16-Q18 [assertion-reason] Cronbach and Snow's aptitude-treatment interaction: a method's effect depends on its interaction with learner characteristics, so the same method gives different results with different learners; no method is best for all.
- S19-Q24 [scenario] When anxious students do better with structured teaching and less anxious with unstructured, with equal overall means, method and learner trait interact (ATI); no method is best for all.

## 3.4 Factors related to support material  (4 booklet questions)

### Core must teach

## 3.5 Factors related to instructional facilities  (6 booklet questions)

### Core must teach
- S5-Q16 [single] Instructional facilities are physical resources an institution provides (classrooms, labs, libraries, projectors, internet). Humour is a teacher trait, aptitude a learner trait, syllabus policy institutional.
- S8-Q18 [scenario] Instructional facilities are enabling conditions; their effect depends on teacher skill and readiness, so institutions must give training and support along with equipment.
- S11-Q44 [scenario] Instructional facilities are infrastructure provided by the institution (networks, terminals, labs, libraries); support material is the learning content and aids (e.g. e-journals). Availability of material is useless without facilities to access it.
- S13-Q7 [scenario] Laboratories provide handling of apparatus, measuring and observing, so their absence prevents psychomotor (manipulative skill) objectives; cognitive knowledge can still be partly gained from lectures. Instructional facilities limit achievable objectives.
- S16-Q32 [assertion-reason] Libraries are instructional facilities, but they go unused when teaching does not require independent reading, hours are inconvenient, or students lack library skills; classification does not explain use.
- S17-Q35 [two-statement] Providing ICT facilities does not by itself change teaching methods; use depends on teacher skill, willingness, time and institutional support. Access is necessary but not sufficient.

## 3.6 Factors related to the learning environment  (12 booklet questions)

### Core must teach
- S1-Q20 [single] A positive climate shows mutual respect, warmth, open communication and shared purpose where students feel safe to err. Fear, public ranking/constant competition and minimal interaction raise anxiety and harm learning.
- S2-Q48 [single] Learning environment has a physical side (space, light, air, furniture, noise) and a psychological side (relationships, warmth, freedom from fear).
- S3-Q26 [single] Noise is a physical barrier in the learning environment that competes for attention; it does not change intelligence or aptitude, which are stable learner traits.
- S4-Q50 [single] Seeing one another's faces encourages student-to-student talk, so circular/U-shaped seating suits discussion; rows and tiered halls focus attention to the front and suit lectures.
- S5-Q20 [single] Psychological/socio-emotional climate depends on relationships; fear of ridicule or punishment silences students even if room and curriculum are fine.
- S14-Q45 [single] Treating errors as learning steps rather than ridiculing them builds psychological safety, so students participate and attempt hard tasks. It differs from laissez-faire (withdrawal of guidance) and from authoritarian or competitive climates.
- S15-Q36 [scenario] The Hawthorne effect is improved performance due to awareness of being studied or given attention. Pygmalion works through teacher expectations; John Henry effect is the control group working harder to compete; halo effect is a rating bias spreading from one trait.
- S17-Q9 [assertion-reason] Research favours cooperative over competitive classroom structures for achievement and relationships, but moderate friendly competition (between groups or against one's own record) can motivate.
- S17-Q48 [scenario] Halo effect: favourable impression on one trait spreads to rating of unrelated traits. Leniency error: rating everyone high. Central tendency error: rating everyone near the middle.
- S19-Q26 [assertion-reason] Excessive noise is a physical-environment factor; in communication terms it is a barrier that distorts or blocks reception of the teacher's message and diverts attention.

### Going Further must teach
- S11-Q1 [assertion-reason] A threatening (psychological) climate lowers participation because students fear ridicule or punishment. Physical conditions such as lighting and ventilation also affect attention, but they are a separate component of the environment.
- S13-Q1 [assertion-reason] Seating affects interaction: students in the front and centre ('action zone') interact most with the teacher, and circular seating increases student-to-student talk; back-row students do not participate more.

## 3.7 Factors related to the institution  (11 booklet questions)

### Core must teach
- S1-Q15 [single] As class size grows, individual guidance, interaction and feedback suffer; lectures, announcements and common tests can still be given to large groups.
- S2-Q38 [single] Institution-related factors include academic calendar, timetable, workload, class size, rules and administrative support, which set limits on teacher planning.
- S3-Q45 [single] The Board of Studies (teachers plus external experts) frames and revises the syllabus, which goes to the Academic Council for approval; finance, building and selection committees have other roles.
- S4-Q27 [single] Admissions, sections, class size, timetable and teaching load are fixed by institutional policy; examples, questions and tone of voice are the teacher's choices.
- S6-Q27 [single] Institutional leadership and climate are institution-related factors; supportive heads give freedom, resources and professional development, but do not replace planning, judgement or evaluation.
- S8-Q24 [single] When everything depends on final exams, teaching narrows to likely questions ('teaching to the test'), an institution-related constraint on methods.
- S10-Q32 [single] Workload is an institution-related factor; clerical/administrative duties take time from preparation, feedback and student contact and add fatigue.
- S11-Q38 [multi] Institution-related factors include class size (admission and sectioning policies), evaluation policy, timetable, resources and administration. Subject mastery is a teacher-related factor, though the institution can support it.
- S13-Q29 [assertion-reason] Institutional climate (leadership, recognition, participation in decisions, staff relations) shapes teacher morale. The institution also fixes the academic calendar, but that function does not explain morale.
- S18-Q50 [single] NAAC recommends each institution set up an IQAC to plan, guide and monitor quality; it develops benchmarks, collects stakeholder feedback and prepares the AQAR. Academic Council approves courses and regulations; Board of Studies handles subject syllabi.
- S19-Q18 [multi] Institution-related factors (in all institutions, public or private) include class size and teacher-student ratio, assessment policy that encourages or discourages innovation, and faculty development opportunities.

## 5.1 Teaching aids: purposes, classification and Dale's Cone of Experience  (15 booklet questions)

### Core must teach
- S1-Q21 [single] Edgar Dale (1946) arranged learning experiences in a cone from direct purposeful experience (base) to verbal symbols (top); concrete supports abstract and guides choice of aids.
- S3-Q25 [single] Aids classified by sense: audio (radio, tape recorder, podcast), visual (charts, models, globes), audio-visual (television, films).
- S6-Q6 [single] Dale's Cone base = direct purposeful experiences (most concrete); moving up through contrived experiences, demonstrations, exhibits, motion pictures, visual symbols to verbal symbols at the apex (most abstract).
- S9-Q37 [single] Dale's Cone bottom to top: direct experiences, contrived experiences, dramatised experiences, demonstrations, study trips, exhibits, (TV), motion pictures, recordings/radio/still pictures, visual symbols, verbal symbols.
- S10-Q8 [single] Audio aids: tape recorder, radio; visual aids: filmstrip, flannel board, charts; audio-visual: sound film, television; more senses usually aid retention.
- S12-Q35 [single] An aid teaches only when integrated with the lesson: the teacher previews it, sets guiding questions, shows the relevant part and follows with discussion linked to objectives. Mere screening, or replacing the lesson, leaves learning to chance.
- S12-Q39 [sequence] Dale's cone from concrete base to abstract top: direct purposeful experiences, contrived experiences, dramatised experiences, demonstrations, study trips, exhibits, television, motion pictures, recordings/radio/still pictures, visual symbols, verbal symbols.
- S15-Q5 [single] Non-projected aids are seen directly: chalkboard, charts, models, flash cards, flannel board. Filmstrips and overhead transparencies need a projector; the epidiascope is itself a projector for opaque objects and slides.
- S15-Q22 [sequence] Dale's cone base upward: direct purposeful experiences, contrived experiences, dramatised experiences, demonstrations, study trips, exhibits, television, motion pictures, recordings/radio/still pictures, visual symbols, verbal symbols (apex).
- S18-Q43 [single] Projected aids are thrown on a screen by a projector: slides, filmstrips, transparencies, films, opaque projection. Non-projected: flash cards, charts, flannel boards, models, bulletin boards.
- S20-Q46 [scenario] Aid choice depends on objective and audience: showing motion to a large class needs a projected animation; specimens, charts and flash cards are static and small.

### Going Further must teach
- S14-Q1 [single] Dale's cone is read in three parts matching Bruner: bottom bands (direct, contrived, dramatised experiences) are doing/enactive; middle bands (demonstrations to still pictures) are observing/iconic; top bands (visual and verbal symbols) are symbolising/symbolic.
- S17-Q7 [single] Dale's cone from base: direct purposeful experiences, contrived experiences, dramatised experiences, demonstrations, study trips, exhibits, television, motion pictures, recordings/radio/still pictures, visual symbols, verbal symbols.
- S19-Q44 [single] Dale's cone ranks experiences by abstraction and carried no numbers; popular retention percentages (10% read, 20% heard...) were added by others and lack research support.
- S20-Q34 [assertion-reason] Cognitive load theory: working memory handles few elements at once; excessive aids or stimuli overload it and divert attention from the objective, hindering learning.

## 5.2 Traditional support  (10 booklet questions)

### Core must teach
- S1-Q39 [single] Chalkboard is the oldest traditional aid needing no electricity or device. Smart boards, webinar tools and learning apps are ICT-based. Support systems grouped as traditional, modern (audio-visual), ICT-based.
- S4-Q6 [single] Three-dimensional aids (models, specimens, objects, globes, mock-ups) have length, breadth and depth; charts, maps and photographs are two-dimensional. Models help when the real object is too big, small or costly.
- S5-Q45 [single] Flash cards carry a word, picture, formula or question shown briefly; suit drill, revision and recall; simple non-projected visual aids.
- S8-Q2 [single] Chalkboard: cheap, always available, needs no power, diagrams grow step by step; limits: loss of eye contact while writing, content rubbed off and not stored.
- S9-Q5 [single] Good handouts give structure, key terms, diagrams so students listen and think; they support but do not replace lecture or further reading.
- S11-Q18 [single] The chalkboard suits content that unfolds during the lesson (derivations, step-by-step working) at students' pace and can be altered in response to questions. Photographs, recordings and large prepared tables are better shown with projected or digital aids.
- S13-Q36 [two-statement] A model is an enlarged, reduced or simplified representation used when the real object is too large, small or inaccessible. A specimen is a real object or sample representing its class (rock, leaf, insect).
- S18-Q21 [assertion-reason] Traditional aids (charts, models, chalkboard) remain useful: inexpensive, need no electricity, under teacher's control, models can be handled. Good teaching combines traditional and digital support.
- S20-Q41 [assertion-reason] A flannel board lets cut-outs backed with rough material stick, be added, moved or removed, so displays build up step by step; it is a non-projected aid, but that classification does not explain this advantage.

### Going Further must teach
- S16-Q43 [single] Diorama: three-dimensional miniature scene with figures before a painted background. Mock-up: simplified working replica for training. Cut-away model: shows the inside of an object. Specimen: real object or sample.

## 5.3 Modern support  (5 booklet questions)

### Core must teach
- S2-Q49 [single] OHP projects an enlarged image of a transparent sheet onto a screen while the teacher faces the class; a projected visual aid now largely replaced by LCD projectors.
- S9-Q1 [single] Language lab: audio/computer stations where learners listen to model speech, record and compare responses with teacher monitoring; for oral-aural skills.
- S13-Q19 [single] The OHP projects transparencies brightly in a lit room with the teacher facing the class. Slide projectors need a darkened room; opaque objects need an episcope/epidiascope; OHP needs electricity and shows no motion or sound.
- S16-Q14 [match] Episcope projects opaque pictures/objects by reflected light; slide projector (diascope) projects small mounted transparent slides; OHP projects large writable transparencies; LCD projector shows computer output.

### Going Further must teach
- S7-Q41 [single] Epidiascope = episcope (opaque material by reflected light) + diascope (transparent slides); projected visual aid needing darkened room.

## 5.4 ICT-based support  (19 booklet questions)

### Core must teach
- S2-Q40 [single] ICT = Information and Communication Technology: digital tools to create, store, send and receive information (computers, internet, projectors, mobiles, software).
- S3-Q5 [single] An LMS is software to deliver and manage online courses (materials, assignments, quizzes, forums, grades); examples Moodle, Google Classroom, Canvas, Blackboard.
- S3-Q32 [single] An LCD/LED projector connected to a computer displays slides, video and web pages for the class; printers make paper copies, scanners digitise paper, webcams capture video.
- S4-Q20 [single] Open Educational Resources are materials in any medium in the public domain or under open licence that anyone may use, adapt and redistribute free (open courseware, open textbooks, many SWAYAM materials).
- S5-Q1 [single] Webinar = web + seminar: a live online session where a speaker presents to remote participants who ask questions via chat/audio; synchronous ICT mode. Not a recording, a listing website or a printed report.
- S6-Q30 [single] Smart classroom: computer, projector/interactive board, audio, internet for multimedia in face-to-face teaching; teacher remains central.
- S6-Q44 [single] Moodle (Modular Object-Oriented Dynamic Learning Environment) is free and open source; Blackboard is commercial; Google Classroom and MS Teams are proprietary.
- S7-Q32 [scenario] Video conferencing gives synchronous two-way audio-video among several places; podcasts and screen recordings are one-way asynchronous; e-mail lacks immediacy.
- S8-Q41 [single] Multimedia combines text, images, sound, video and animation, usually computer-controlled and interactive; addresses several senses.
- S9-Q3 [single] Podcast = audio/video files over internet for on-demand (asynchronous) listening; webinar = live online seminar; blog = web journal; wiki = collaboratively edited site.
- S10-Q10 [single] Wiki: pages any authorised user creates/edits via browser with change history; podcasts, webcasts, screencasts are one-to-many audio/video delivery.
- S12-Q44 [assertion-reason] ICT can combine text, sound, image and animation, but its effect depends on how the teacher designs and uses it; poorly used slides or videos can make learners passive, so ICT does not automatically ensure better learning.
- S13-Q26 [single] A learning management system (e.g. Moodle) adds enrolment, assignments with deadlines, auto-scored quizzes, grade books and progress tracking and reporting to file storage and sharing; tracking is its distinguishing feature.
- S20-Q10 [assertion-reason] An LMS automates distribution of material and scoring of objective quizzes, freeing the teacher for design, qualitative feedback, discussion and individual support; it does not remove the need for a teacher.

### Going Further must teach
- S14-Q7 [scenario] SAMR (Puentedura): Substitution (technology replaces a tool, no functional change), Augmentation (functional improvement, e.g. spell-check), Modification (task significantly redesigned), Redefinition (previously inconceivable tasks).
- S17-Q49 [single] BY = attribution required; SA = share-alike (adaptations under same licence); NC = non-commercial; ND = no derivatives. Open licences remove the need for individual permission.
- S18-Q7 [two-statement] Mayer's coherence principle: extraneous material, even attractive, reduces learning. Modality principle: graphics are better with spoken narration than on-screen text.
- S18-Q9 [scenario] Tutorial mode teaches new material through dialogue and, with remedial routing, resembles Crowder's branching programming. Drill and practice rehearses taught material; simulation models a real system.
- S19-Q37 [single] Self-learning materials are self-explanatory, self-contained, self-directed, self-motivating and self-evaluating; in-text check-your-progress questions with answers serve self-evaluation. ## Booklet 0 (diagnostic) questions the guide must also teach
- P2-Q38 -> 5.1: answer and explanation: (C)**, because Dale's Cone of Experience arranges learning experiences from the most concrete at the base to the most abstract at the top. Direct purposeful experience, in which the learner actually does the thing, forms the base. Verbal symbols, that is, words alone, sit at the tip, with visual symbols just below them. 
- P4-Q30 -> 3.3: answer and explanation: (D)**, because learner-related factors are those that lie in the students themselves: what they already know (1), how motivated they are (2) and their aptitude for the subject (5). The teacher's mastery of the subject (3) is a teacher-related factor, and the seating arrangement (4) belongs to the learning environment.
