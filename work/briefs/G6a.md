
## 6.1 Elements of evaluation  (14 booklet questions)

### Core must teach
- S1-Q35 [single] Measurement quantifies (e.g., 72/100); testing and scoring obtain that number. Evaluation judges the worth of performance against objectives and is the broadest term.
- S2-Q6 [single] Measurement assigns numbers by rules and answers 'how much'; it does not judge worth. Judgement, which may use qualitative information, belongs to evaluation.
- S3-Q7 [sequence] Measurement assigns numbers; assessment gathers and interprets numerical and descriptive information about learning; evaluation judges worth. From narrowest to broadest: measurement, assessment, evaluation.
- S4-Q11 [single] A plain quantity ('68 out of 100') is measurement; adding a value judgement ('good for his class') makes it evaluation; appraisal is another word for judgement.
- S8-Q16 [single] Measurement reports quantities (marks, items correct, time); evaluation interprets and judges worth (e.g. 'shows very good progress'); evaluation = measurement + value judgement.  FLAG: WRONG?: explanation says 'the first statement interprets a score' but the key (C) is the third option; wording of the explanation is inconsistent with the key and should read 'option (C)'.
- S9-Q38 [two-statement] Assessment collects evidence (tests, observation, assignments) and interprets it; evaluation judges worth against objectives or standards and includes assessment.
- S10-Q16 [sequence] Evaluation starts with formulating objectives, then providing learning experiences, applying evaluation tools, interpreting results; objectives-learning experiences-evaluation triangle.
- S12-Q25 [single] Following Tyler, the evaluation triangle links objectives (intended changes), learning experiences (to produce them) and evaluation (how far they occurred); each influences the others.
- S14-Q10 [scenario] Evaluation functions: instructional (feedback to teaching and learning), administrative (promotion, selection, certification), guidance (educational and vocational choices), research (data for studies).
- S14-Q42 [single] Gronlund: evaluation = quantitative description (measurement) + qualitative description (non-measurement) + value judgements about desirability. Judgement of worth turns description into evaluation.
- S15-Q34 [two-statement] Measurement assigns numbers according to rules and is quantitative; evaluation is wider, using quantitative measurement and qualitative description and adding a value judgement against objectives.
- S16-Q40 [sequence] A test is an instrument; measurement is the process of assigning numbers to performance; evaluation interprets numbers with other evidence and judges worth against objectives.
- S20-Q5 [two-statement] Learning is inferred from performance on a sample of tasks, so measurement is indirect; sampling, examinee condition and scoring introduce error; obtained score = true score + error, hence reliability and standard error of measurement.

### Going Further must teach
- S20-Q28 [single] CIPP (Daniel Stufflebeam): Context (needs, goals), Input (resources, plans), Process (implementation), Product (outcomes); decision-oriented programme evaluation.  FLAG: NEW?: programme evaluation models (CIPP) not in outline; add to 6.1 or 6.3.

## 6.2 Qualities of a good test  (27 booklet questions)

### Core must teach
- S1-Q9 [single] A reliable test gives consistent results on re-administration under similar conditions. Validity asks whether the test measures what it claims to measure; economy/usability concerns practicality.
- S2-Q3 [single] Objectivity means different examiners (or same examiner at different times) give the same score; MCQs highly objective, essays less. Objectivity supports reliability. Validity = measures what it claims.
- S2-Q29 [single] Validity, the most important test quality, is whether a test measures what it claims; reliability is consistency, objectivity is freedom from scorer bias, practicability is usability.
- S3-Q36 [single] Qualities of a good test: validity, reliability, objectivity, usability; usability/practicability means the test can be given, scored and interpreted without undue time, cost or difficulty.
- S3-Q43 [single] Content validity is how well items represent course content and objectives, ensured by a blueprint distributing marks over topics and objectives. Predictive validity forecasts future performance; retest reliability is consistency over time.
- S4-Q34 [single] Predictive validity (a criterion-related validity) is how well scores foretell later performance, essential for entrance/aptitude tests. Content validity = syllabus coverage; face validity = looks appropriate; internal consistency is a reliability measure.
- S6-Q25 [single] Validity (measuring what it intends) is most important; reliability is necessary but not sufficient for validity; objectivity and usability are other qualities.
- S6-Q41 [single] Reliability is estimated by correlating two sets of scores (test-retest, split-half, parallel forms); coefficient ranges 0-1, about 0.80+ expected of standardised tests.
- S8-Q25 [single] Longer tests sample ability more fully so chance errors cancel; Spearman-Brown formula relates test length and reliability; very short tests are unreliable.
- S8-Q28 [scenario] When reading ability affects scores in a maths test, something other than the intended ability is measured, lowering validity even if scoring stays objective.
- S10-Q50 [single] Construct validity: degree scores reflect a theoretical construct (creativity, intelligence, anxiety), shown by correlations and factor analysis; content validity = sampling of content; predictive = forecasting; face = appearance.
- S13-Q22 [scenario] Reliability is consistency; validity is measuring what the test claims. A test can be reliable but not valid (consistently measuring the wrong thing), but cannot be valid without being reliable.
- S15-Q2 [match] Content validity: items correspond to syllabus/blueprint. Predictive validity: scores correlate with a later criterion. Test-retest reliability: correlation of the same test given twice. Inter-rater reliability: agreement between independent scorers.
- S16-Q8 [single] Test-retest gives coefficient of stability; parallel forms give equivalence; split-half and Kuder-Richardson give internal consistency; parallel forms after an interval give stability and equivalence.
- S16-Q35 [match] Content validity: adequate sampling of the course. Concurrent: agreement with a criterion available now. Predictive: forecasting later performance. Construct: reflects the theoretical trait.

### Going Further must teach
- S4-Q44 [single] Split-half: test given once, divided into halves (odd/even), scores correlated and corrected with Spearman-Brown formula to estimate reliability; other methods are test-retest and parallel forms.
- S5-Q17 [single] Test-retest gives the same test twice and correlates scores (stability). Split-half and rational equivalence (Kuder-Richardson) use one administration (internal consistency); parallel forms use two equivalent versions.
- S5-Q38 [single] Face validity is the surface impression of relevance to candidates/lay observers, not technical validity; content, predictive and construct validity require systematic evidence.
- S11-Q29 [single] Kuder-Richardson formulas (KR-20, KR-21) estimate internal consistency from one administration of a test with items scored 1/0. Test-retest needs two administrations; parallel forms needs two versions; inter-rater compares scorers; split-half also uses one administration.
- S12-Q20 [scenario] Criterion-related validity is concurrent when the criterion is measured at the same time (e.g. established test in same week) and predictive when the criterion lies in the future. Content validity concerns sampling of content; construct validity concerns the underlying trait.
- S13-Q24 [single] The standard error of measurement estimates spread of obtained scores around a person's true score (e.g. 60 ± 3 gives 57-63); higher reliability gives smaller SEM. Standard deviation describes spread across a group.
- S17-Q4 [numerical] Maximum validity coefficient = square root of reliability coefficient (index of reliability); e.g. reliability 0.64 gives maximum validity 0.80.
- S17-Q43 [single] Split-half (odd-even) gives spuriously high reliability on speed tests because halves are nearly identical; test-retest or parallel forms should be used for speeded tests. Power tests can use split-half.
- S18-Q11 [scenario] Replacing written tests with observed performance raises validity (direct evidence) but lowers reliability (small sample, observer variation) and practicability (time); rubrics and multiple observations limit the loss.
- S19-Q19 [numerical] Full-test reliability = 2r/(1 + r) where r is half-test correlation; e.g. 1.20/1.60 = 0.75. Longer tests are more reliable.
- S19-Q35 [scenario] Construct under-representation: test too narrow, omitting important parts of the construct. Construct-irrelevant variance: scores affected by factors outside the construct (e.g. reading speed).
- S20-Q44 [scenario] Reliability coefficients depend on score spread; in a homogeneous group true variance is small, so error forms a larger share and the coefficient falls (restriction of range).

## 6.3 Types of evaluation  (42 booklet questions)

### Core must teach
- S1-Q7 [single] Norm-referenced tests interpret a score relative to a group (ranks, percentiles), as in competitive selection. Criterion-referenced tests compare with a fixed standard regardless of others; self-referenced compares with one's own earlier performance.
- S1-Q14 [single] A diagnostic test identifies where and why a learner goes wrong so remedial teaching can follow. Prognostic tests predict future success, aptitude tests measure potential in a field, intelligence tests measure general mental ability.
- S1-Q29 [single] Formative evaluation happens while teaching is in progress to give feedback and improve learning, not to grade. Summative is at the end; placement is before instruction; external is by outside examiners.
- S2-Q17 [single] Summative evaluation sums up achievement after a unit/term/course, used for grading, promotion and certification. During lessons = formative; before instruction = placement or diagnostic.
- S2-Q23 [single] Placement evaluation is done before instruction to determine entry level and grouping; summative and terminal tests come at the end; retention tests measure what is retained later.
- S2-Q41 [single] Criterion-referenced tests check whether a pre-set standard is reached (e.g., driving test), irrespective of others; norm-referenced compares students with one another.
- S3-Q17 [single] A class quiz given during teaching shows what has been learnt and what needs work (formative). Year-end exams and degree viva are summative; entrance exams are for selection.
- S4-Q42 [scenario] A test where everyone meeting a fixed standard passes, regardless of others, is criterion-referenced; in norm-referenced tests success is limited by rank (competitive exams for fixed seats).
- S4-Q47 [single] Summative evaluation results are used for grades, promotion and certificates; daily feedback is formative; placement and diagnosis are separate types.
- S5-Q21 [single] Diagnosis locates specific weaknesses and causes so the teacher can plan remedial instruction; grading and certification follow summative evaluation.
- S6-Q8 [single] Formative = assessment for learning (used to improve learning during the process); summative = assessment of learning; self-assessment = assessment as learning; placement/diagnostic come before.
- S7-Q28 [single] Norm-referenced: position relative to group (e.g. higher than 85% of class); criterion-referenced: performance against fixed task/standard.
- S7-Q38 [two-statement] A test used to locate weaknesses for re-teaching is formative; term-end exam deciding promotion is summative.
- S7-Q46 [scenario] Diagnostic evaluation seeks causes of persistent learning difficulties and leads to remedial teaching; formative only shows weakness; placement is at entry; summative at end.
- S8-Q19 [single] Formative assessment guides ongoing learning (feedback on drafts); final results, scholarship ranking and entrance tests are decision-making (summative/selection/placement) uses.
- S9-Q33 [match] Placement: entry behaviour before instruction; formative: monitor progress, feedback; diagnostic: causes of persistent difficulties; summative: certify achievement at end.
- S10-Q12 [single] Formative evaluation defined by use: findings improve ongoing teaching and learning; external setting, whole-course coverage and final grades typify summative.
- S10-Q43 [single] Prognostic test forecasts likely future performance in a subject/course (guidance, selection); diagnostic finds causes of present errors; summative certifies achievement.
- S11-Q4 [multi] Formative evaluation gives feedback during learning and may be informal (questioning, observation); summative evaluation is high-stakes and used for grading and certification. The same instrument can be formative or summative depending on how results are used.
- S11-Q45 [scenario] Selecting a fixed number of top scorers is norm-referenced (cut-off floats with rank); passing all who reach a fixed standard is criterion-referenced (number passing floats).
- S12-Q10 [single] Gronlund: formative evaluation gives quick feedback and correction ('first aid'); diagnostic evaluation probes persistent difficulties for underlying causes with specially designed tests to plan remediation. Prognostic evaluation forecasts future success; placement assigns starting position.
- S12-Q24 [scenario] Assessment as learning: students themselves monitor work against criteria and regulate learning. Assessment for learning: teacher uses evidence to adjust teaching. Assessment of learning: records achievement at the end.
- S14-Q8 [assertion-reason] Formative evaluation, conducted while change is possible, gives feedback to the teacher on teaching effectiveness and to the learner on progress. Summative evaluation is used for certification, a separate fact.
- S14-Q17 [scenario] Placement evaluation checks whether learners have the prerequisites and whether they have already achieved the intended outcomes; those already proficient should skip ahead or receive enrichment/advanced work.
- S15-Q8 [scenario] Ipsative assessment judges a learner against his or her own earlier performance, reporting personal progress. Norm-referenced compares with other learners; criterion-referenced compares with a fixed external standard.
- S15-Q25 [assertion-reason] Certifying mastery of minimum competencies needs comparison with a fixed standard (criterion-referenced). Norm-referenced tests are built to spread scores for ranking; rank does not show mastery.
- S15-Q44 [assertion-reason] Formative evaluation monitors progress; diagnostic evaluation probes causes of persistent difficulties. Placement evaluation concerns entry performance before instruction, a different type.
- S16-Q10 [scenario] Feedback that guides further learning is a formative use; counting marks toward the final grade is a summative use; one assessment can serve both functions.
- S16-Q33 [single] Norm-referenced scores locate a student among others (percentile rank); criterion-referenced scores describe mastery of a defined domain (percentage of objectives mastered) without comparison.
- S18-Q3 [scenario] A fixed minimum score that any number can meet is criterion-referenced; selection by rank (top x per cent) is norm-referenced; selection tests often combine both.

## 6.4 Tools and techniques of evaluation; item analysis  (30 booklet questions)

### Core must teach
- S2-Q20 [single] Objective-type items have one predetermined correct answer, sample widely and are quick to score; essay-type items need extended answers marked by judgement.
- S3-Q31 [single] Essay items let students organise and express ideas, testing higher abilities, but have limited sampling and subjective scoring; matching, completion and true-false are objective-type.
- S4-Q4 [single] An MCQ has a stem (the problem), a key (correct option) and distractors (plausible wrong options). Premises and responses are the columns of a matching item.
- S5-Q32 [single] True-false items have two choices, so blind guessing gives 50% chance; four-option MCQ gives 25%; supply-type items (essay, short-answer, completion) leave little room for guessing.
- S6-Q3 [single] An anecdotal record is a brief written description of a significant incident in a student's behaviour (qualitative); test scores, percentile ranks and standard scores are quantitative.
- S6-Q20 [single] Item analysis after tryout finds each item's difficulty (proportion correct), discrimination (high vs low scorers) and distractor functioning.
- S7-Q2 [single] Essay tests reveal ability to select, organise, integrate and express ideas; objective tests are superior in content sampling, scoring objectivity and speed of scoring.
- S7-Q19 [numerical] Difficulty index/facility value = proportion answering correctly; 0.90 = very easy; items near 0.50 discriminate best; very easy or hard items discriminate poorly.
- S8-Q3 [single] Blueprint/table of specifications relates content to objectives (and question forms), ensuring proportional sampling and content validity; marking scheme and scoring key come after items are written.
- S9-Q19 [single] Objective items: supply-type/recall (completion, short-answer) vs selection-type/recognition (true-false, matching, MCQ).
- S10-Q6 [assertion-reason] Essay marks vary across examiners (judgement-dependent scoring); essays have few questions so sample narrowly; objective tests sample widely.
- S10-Q33 [single] NRT ranks examinees, so items answered correctly or wrongly by all add nothing and are dropped; in CRT they may be retained.
- S13-Q6 [match] True-false items allow a 50 per cent chance by guessing; matching items suit only homogeneous associations; essay scoring varies between examiners (subjectivity); MCQs need plausible distractors that are hard to write.
- S14-Q44 [multi] MCQ stems should present a single clear problem; distractors should be plausible to the uninformed; the key's position should be random, not patterned; 'all of the above' type options give clues and are best avoided.
- S17-Q3 [scenario] Checklist records presence/absence; rating scale records degree on a numerical or verbal scale; a rubric adds descriptors of performance at each level (analytic per criterion, holistic overall).
- S17-Q12 [scenario] Cumulative record: institution's year-by-year comprehensive record of a student. Portfolio: student's selection of work with reflection. Anecdotal record: single incidents. Sociogram: choices within a group.
- S18-Q15 [sequence] Order: planning with blueprint, writing items, try-out on a sample, item analysis, assembling the final form.

### Going Further must teach
- S6-Q33 [single] Matching items have premises (first column) and responses (second); more responses than premises reduces guessing; stem and distractors belong to MCQs.
- S9-Q20 [numerical] DI = (upper correct - lower correct)/n per group: (32-12)/40 = 0.50; dividing by 80 gives 0.25 (error); facility value = 44/80 = 0.55.
- S11-Q24 [single] When the key is longer or more qualified than distractors, test-wise examinees pick it, so the item rewards test-wiseness, lowering validity and discrimination. Options should be similar in length, grammar and detail.
- S11-Q42 [numerical] Facility (difficulty) value = (RU + RL) / (NU + NL), e.g. 30/50 = 0.60. Discrimination index = (RU - RL) / N of one group, e.g. 10/25 = 0.40.
- S12-Q14 [single] A good distractor attracts more low scorers than high scorers. If a distractor attracts more high scorers, it may be ambiguous/defensible or the item miskeyed, and the item must be reviewed.
- S14-Q35 [single] Discrimination index = proportion correct in upper group minus that in lower group; a negative value means more low scorers answered correctly, signalling an ambiguous stem, wrong key or misleading option; such items are revised or dropped. Difficulty is shown by facility value.
- S15-Q13 [numerical] Facility value = proportion answering correctly (30/100 = 0.30). If upper and lower groups do equally well, discrimination index is near zero; such an item fails to separate able from weak and needs revision.
- S17-Q16 [numerical] Correction for guessing: S = R - W/(n-1), n = number of options; omitted items are not counted. E.g. 40 - 12/3 = 36.
- S17-Q18 [numerical] Item analysis uses upper and lower 27 per cent of examinees ranked by total score (Kelley); for 200 examinees each group has 54.
- S18-Q41 [numerical] Facility value = (RU + RL)/(NU + NL); discrimination index = (RU - RL)/N of one group. E.g. 33/60 = 0.55 and 15/30 = 0.50.
- S18-Q47 [single] Interpretive exercise: introductory material (passage, table, graph) followed by several objective items testing interpretation and inference. Cloze test deletes words at intervals to measure reading comprehension.
- S20-Q11 [numerical] Expected chance score = number of items x 1/number of options; 50 items with four options gives 12.5.
- S20-Q39 [numerical] A distractor chosen by fewer than 5 per cent of examinees is non-functional; a distractor chosen more than the key is over-attractive and should be checked for ambiguity.

## 6.5 Reading test scores: a method chapter  (11 booklet questions)

### Core must teach
- S3-Q1 [single] A percentile rank gives the percentage of the group scoring below a student, i.e., relative position, which is norm-referenced interpretation. Criterion-referenced and mastery tests report whether a fixed standard is reached.
- S13-Q44 [single] A percentile rank gives the percentage of the reference group scoring below a given score (PR 80 = 80 per cent scored below). It differs from percentage of marks, which compares with the maximum possible.
- S17-Q19 [assertion-reason] A raw score has little meaning alone; it must be interpreted against a norm (others' performance) or criterion (standard) to become information.
- S18-Q35 [scenario] Mean shows general level, standard deviation shows spread; with equal means, the class with larger SD is more heterogeneous and needs more differentiated instruction.

### Going Further must teach
- S11-Q39 [single] Nominal scales label categories; ordinal scales show order without equal differences (ranks); interval scales have equal units; ratio scales have equal units and a true zero.
- S13-Q12 [single] A ratio scale needs an absolute zero meaning complete absence; tests sample only part of a subject, so a score of zero is not zero knowledge and 80 is not twice 40. Scores do show order.
- S14-Q24 [numerical] z = (score - mean) / SD; e.g. (65 - 50) / 10 = 1.5, meaning 1.5 standard deviations above the mean. A z-score is a norm-referenced standard score.
- S18-Q18 [numerical] z = (X - mean)/SD; T = 50 + 10z (mean 50, SD 10). E.g. (67-55)/12 = 1, T = 60.
- S18-Q38 [numerical] In a normal distribution about 34 per cent lie between mean and 1 SD on each side, so about 68 per cent within plus/minus 1 SD and about 95 per cent within plus/minus 2 SD. IQ mean 100, SD 15.
- S19-Q5 [scenario] When examiners differ in mean and SD, raw marks should be converted to standard scores (z or T) before combining; otherwise the examiner with larger spread dominates. This is scaling or moderation.
- S20-Q45 [single] A correlation of +0.60 shows a moderately strong positive relation but not causation; shared variance is r squared (0.36), not 0.60.

## Booklet 0 (diagnostic) questions the guide must also teach
- P1-Q30 -> 6.3: answer and explanation: (C)**, because Statement I correctly defines a criterion-referenced test: performance is judged against a fixed standard of mastery. Statement II is false: a norm-referenced test ranks each learner against the performance of others, for example by percentile. The tempting error is to accept Statement II, which actually repeats the description of a criterion-referenced test under the wrong label. 
- P2-Q6 -> 6.3: answer and explanation: (C)**, because summative evaluation comes at the end of a course or term and is used to grade, certify or promote learners. Formative evaluation takes place during instruction to improve learning, diagnostic evaluation looks for the causes of persistent learning difficulties, and placement evaluation is carried out before instruction to decide where a learner should begin. 
- P3-Q38 -> 6.3: answer and explanation: (A)**, because both statements are true and the reason explains the assertion. Diagnostic evaluation looks for the particular points at which a learner is going wrong and the causes behind them. Its purpose is to make remedial teaching possible, and remedial teaching cannot be planned until those causes are known. Diagnosis comes first; remedy follows. 
- P4-Q14 -> 6.3: answer and explanation: (A)**, because both statements are true. Formative assessment takes place while teaching is in progress, through questions, quizzes, class tests and observation. Its purpose is to give feedback to learner and teacher, so that gaps are noticed and teaching is adjusted before the course ends. It is assessment for learning, as distinct from assessment of learning. 
- P5-Q30 -> 6.2: answer and explanation: (B)**, because consistency of scores is reliability, and measuring what the test is meant to measure is validity. This test is consistent but off target, so it is reliable but not valid, like a weighing scale that always reads two kilograms too high. 'Valid but not reliable' is tempting, yet it describes something that cannot occur: a test that gives inconsistent scores cannot be measuring its target accurately.
