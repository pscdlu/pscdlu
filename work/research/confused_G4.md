|Approach / method|Approach is the underlying view of learning; method is the organised way of teaching a lesson|4.1|
|Method / technique|Method organises a whole lesson; technique is a small device inside it (questioning, buzz group)|4.1|
|Facilitator / transmitter|Facilitator guides active learners (learner-centred); transmitter delivers content to listeners (teacher-centred)|4.1|
|Inductive / deductive|Inductive: examples to rule, slower, discovery; deductive: rule to examples, quicker, practice|4.1|
|Lecture / demonstration|Lecture: learners hear; demonstration: learners also see a process performed; neither gives hands-on practice|4.2|
|Demonstration / laboratory practice|Demonstration: teacher performs; practice: each learner performs; skills come from practice|4.2|
|Economy / participation (lecture)|Lecture is economical (many learners per teacher) but not participatory|4.2|
|Immediate confirmation / self-pacing|Knowing at once whether a response is right / going at one's own speed|4.2|
|Linear / branching programming|Linear (Skinner): small frames, constructed responses, same path; branching (Crowder): larger frames, multiple choice, remedial frames, path depends on response|4.2|
|Mathetics / linear programming|Mathetics (Gilbert): backward chaining, last step first; linear: forward small steps|4.2|
|Seminar / symposium|Seminar: one paper discussed in depth; symposium: several speakers on different aspects in turn|4.3|
|Symposium / panel discussion|Symposium: prepared talks, no conversation among speakers; panel: informal conversation under a moderator|4.3|
|Seminar / workshop|Seminar: paper and discussion; workshop: practical work and a product|4.3|
|Seminar / conference|Conference is larger and broader, with plenary and parallel sessions; both include papers|4.3|
|Chairperson / moderator / rapporteur / convener|Chairperson conducts seminar or symposium; moderator conducts panel; rapporteur records and reports; convener organises|4.3|
|Tutorial / lecture|Tutorial: small group, remedial, supplements; lecture: large group, presentation|4.3|
|Team teaching / peer tutoring|Several teachers share one course / students teach students|4.3|
|Buzz group / fishbowl|Buzz: many small groups talk at once briefly; fishbowl: inner circle talks, outer circle observes|4.3|
|Forum / panel|Forum: the audience talks from the floor; panel: experts on the platform talk|4.3|
|Positive / negative interdependence|Cooperation: each succeeds only if others succeed; competition: one succeeds only if others fail|4.3|
|Jigsaw / STAD|Jigsaw: members teach one another their parts; STAD: team scores from individual improvement points|4.3|
