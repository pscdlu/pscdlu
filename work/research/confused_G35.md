| pair | deciding difference | section |
|---|---|---|
| Support material vs instructional facilities | Material is made or chosen for a particular content (chart, worksheet, slides, e-journal); facilities are lasting infrastructure the institution provides for all teaching (laboratory, library, projector, network) | 3.1, 3.4, 3.5 |
| Learning environment vs instructional facilities | Environment is the condition of the surroundings (light, noise, seating, climate); facilities are the equipment and spaces themselves | 3.1, 3.5 |
| Necessary vs sufficient condition | Necessary: cannot succeed without it; sufficient: enough on its own. Subject mastery and access to facilities are necessary, not sufficient | 3.1, 3.5 |
| Pygmalion effect vs Golem effect | Same self-fulfilling mechanism; high expectations raise performance (Pygmalion), low expectations lower it (Golem) | 3.2 |
| Pygmalion effect vs Hawthorne effect | Pygmalion works through the teacher's expectations; Hawthorne through the subjects' awareness of being observed | 3.2, 3.6 |
| Pygmalion effect vs halo effect | Pygmalion changes actual performance; halo changes only the rater's judgement | 3.2, 3.6 |
| Teacher efficacy vs teacher autonomy | Efficacy is belief in one's capacity to bring about learning; autonomy is freedom to take professional decisions | 3.2 |
| Maturation vs learning | Maturation comes from growth with age, independent of training; learning comes from experience and practice | 3.3 |
| Aptitude vs prior knowledge | Aptitude is potential to learn; prior knowledge is what has already been learned | 3.3 |
| Cognitive vs affective learner factors | Intelligence, aptitude, prior knowledge are cognitive; interest, attitude, motivation, anxiety are affective | 3.3 |
| Cultural capital vs aptitude | Cultural capital is familiarity with educated culture gained at home; aptitude is the potential to learn | 3.3 |
| Suitability vs accuracy of support material | Suitability concerns the learners' level; accuracy concerns the truth of the content; separate criteria, neither explains the other | 3.4 |
| Physical environment vs psychological climate | Light, air, noise, seating are physical; relationships, warmth, fear are psychological | 3.6 |
| Psychological safety vs laissez-faire climate | Safety keeps guidance and high expectations; laissez-faire withdraws guidance | 3.6 |
| Hawthorne effect vs John Henry effect | Hawthorne: the studied group improves because it is observed; John Henry: the control group works harder to compete | 3.6 |
| Halo effect vs leniency error vs central tendency error | Halo: one trait colours other ratings of the same person; leniency: everyone rated high; central tendency: everyone rated near the middle | 3.6 |
| Board of Studies vs Academic Council | Board of Studies frames the syllabus of one subject; Academic Council approves courses, syllabi and regulations for the university | 3.7 |
| Academic Council vs IQAC | Academic Council approves academic matters; IQAC plans, guides and monitors quality and prepares the AQAR | 3.7 |
| Institutional climate vs classroom climate | Institutional climate (leadership, recognition, staff relations) shapes teacher morale; classroom climate shapes student participation | 3.7 |
| Study trip vs demonstration (Dale's cone) | Demonstrations lie below study trips; a study trip is more abstract in Dale's order | 5.1 |
| Visual symbols vs verbal symbols | Visual symbols (charts, maps, graphs) lie just below verbal symbols (words), which form the apex | 5.1 |
| Visual symbols vs still pictures | A photograph resembles its object (still pictures band, iconic); a graph is an abstract sign (visual symbols, symbolic) | 5.1 |
| Projected vs non-projected aids | Projected aids need a projector to throw an image on a screen (epidiascope, OHP, slides, filmstrip); non-projected are seen directly (chalkboard, charts, flash cards, flannel board, models) | 5.1 |
| Audio vs audio-visual aids | Radio and tape recorder address hearing only; television and sound film address both; a silent filmstrip is visual | 5.1 |
| Model vs specimen | A model represents the real thing (enlarged, reduced or simplified); a specimen is the real thing in sample form | 5.2 |
| Diorama vs mock-up vs cut-away model | Diorama: miniature scene before a painted background; mock-up: simplified working replica for training; cut-away: shows the inside | 5.2 |
| Chalkboard vs prepared slide | Chalkboard suits content that unfolds during the lesson; slide suits content prepared in advance | 5.2 |
| Flannel board vs chart | Both non-projected visual aids; only the flannel board lets a display be built up and rearranged | 5.2 |
| Episcope vs diascope | Episcope projects opaque material by reflected light; diascope (slide projector) projects transparent slides | 5.3 |
| Epidiascope vs OHP | Epidiascope: opaque objects and slides, darkened room; OHP: large transparencies, lit room, teacher faces class | 5.3 |
| Webinar vs podcast | Webinar is live (synchronous); podcast is on demand (asynchronous) | 5.4 |
| Wiki vs blog | Wiki is jointly edited with change history; blog is a journal of dated posts | 5.4 |
| Video conferencing vs webcast | Video conferencing is two-way among several places; webcast is one-way from one source to many | 5.4 |
| LMS vs file-sharing service | Both store files; only an LMS tracks and reports each learner's progress | 5.4 |
| Open source vs free of charge | Open source code may be modified and shared (Moodle); a free service may still be proprietary (Google Classroom) | 5.4 |
| CC SA vs ND vs NC | SA: adaptations under the same licence; ND: no adaptations distributed; NC: no commercial use | 5.4 |
| SAMR Substitution vs Augmentation | Substitution: no functional change; Augmentation: functional improvement (e.g. spell-check) | 5.4 |
| Self-contained vs self-evaluating (SLM) | Self-contained: needs no outside sources; self-evaluating: in-text questions with answers let the learner check progress | 5.4 |
